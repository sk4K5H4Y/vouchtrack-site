How it works in an eye care practice

Patient checks out

After exams and frame pickups, every patient automatically gets a review request by text or email — timed for the moments they’re happiest. A QR code by the frame wall catches browsers too.

Reviews roll in

Most patients asked will leave a review; the difference between 30 reviews and 300 is simply asking every patient, every time, automatically.

New patients book

When someone searches “eye doctor near me” or checks the name their insurance listed, your rating and recent reviews make you the practice they choose.

Practice owners ask us

Is this HIPAA-safe?

Asking is fine — patients choose what to share. The replies are where practices get burned, and ours are HIPAA-aware by default: never confirming someone was a patient, never mentioning prescriptions or conditions, always human-approved before posting.

Most of my patients come through insurance lists. Do reviews matter?

Insurance gets you listed; reviews get you picked. Patients with a list of five in-network names choose by star rating almost every time — a strong profile is how you win patients you’d otherwise never meet.

What about complaints over glasses pricing or insurance?

Those frustrations get a private feedback path to you first, where a quick call usually fixes them. Public negatives that appear get calm, professional replies that never argue billing in public — which reads as competence to everyone else.

How AI decides which practice to name

Patients ask about wait times, insurance and frame selection long before they ask about clinical quality. Assistants find those answers in your reviews, or they find nothing and move on to a practice where they can.

Three things decide it: your Google Business Profile, your review signals, and what the rest of the web says about you. We show you where you stand on all three, name the practices being recommended in your place, and then do the work.
